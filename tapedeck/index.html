Tape Deck Features Help

Your music, on tape.

Tape Deck plays your own music as a cassette. It's drawn to scale, the reels wind as you listen, the label's handwritten and the keys latch.

iPhone · iPad · Apple Watch · Mac · CarPlay

Drawn off a real tape

I measured it off a real HF 60 and drew it to the standard's dimensions. The reels are sized by how long the side is and wind across as the music plays.

  • Every album gets a label: title and artist in handwriting, the cover, the running time and a strip with a mark for each track. Click the strip to wind there.
  • Six shells and eight classic tapes. One shell takes its colour from the album cover (and so does the page).
  • 698 tapes copied from real cassettes from the 70s to the 90s, with thirteen label styles. Pick one from a grid of little cassettes, search by name or ID, favourites first. Or set Random, by album and let every album keep its own. Flick across the label for the next one.
  • Eight hands, from a biro to a marker, block capitals to a typed label. Or a different one for every album.
  • Type I, II or IV, Dolby B or C. It's on the label, cut into the shell and you can hear it.
  • Take it apart. A clear shell, the front off or the whole thing exploded into layers that tilt when you move the mouse or the phone.
  • LED VU meters if you want them. Twelve lights a channel, green, amber and red.
  • Each tape remembers where it was. Put an album back in and it carries on from the same track and second. Or turn that off and start every tape from the top.

Keys that behave like a deck's

Portable recorder keys or modern player keys, your call. Either way the tape moves like a tape.

Play latches

It stays down while the tape runs. Press it again and the tape turns over where it is, like the DIR key on a deck.

Wind it

Rewind and fast forward race the reels, with that sound of tape flying past the heads. Or grab the cassette and wind it by hand.

Auto-reverse

Side B plays backwards with a clunk from the mechanism. Or the tape gets turned over between sides, if you'd rather.

Leader and hiss

Every side starts and ends with clear leader you can hear. Shop-bought tapes even have the cue tone after the last track.

Motor wow

The motor spins up when you press Play and winds down when you stop. The pitch bends with it.

Gapless

Albums that run together play as one, joined on the exact sample.

From fresh to chewed

One slider for how battered you want the tape. Turn it up and the hiss comes in, the pitch starts to wobble and the shell picks up scratches.

  • Played in, well loved, worn. Each step sounds and looks a bit older.
  • Chewed. Every so often the deck eats the tape, the music garbles and a loop of tape spills out over the screen. Wind it back in with a pencil (yes, really).
  • Snapped. A track that never turns up pulls the tape tight until it breaks. Press Play to splice it.

Your whole library

Tape Deck plays music you own. Copy it into the app, play a folder where it sits (a NAS is fine) or point it at your own music server. That can be a Tape Deck Server (free, open source, serves a folder of albums from any computer), Plex, Jellyfin or a Subsonic server like Navidrome.

Got Apple Music? Your Apple Music library plays as tapes too.

Recent first

What you played last, then what's new.

Albums, artists, tracks

One button to sort, jump by letter, search as you type. It doesn't mind a missing accent.

Favourites everywhere

Heart a track or a whole album and it follows you to your iPhone, iPad and Mac through iCloud.

Downloads

From a server, an album downloads as you play it. Or grab it ahead of time for the train, the whole album or one track. You choose how much space it gets. The Lock Screen and Dynamic Island show how it's going.

Songs on this iPhone

Switch it on and the songs you synced from a Mac or PC play as tapes too. Copy-protected songs are skipped and the app tells you how many.

Through iCloud Drive

Drop albums in the Tape Deck folder in iCloud Drive and they're on your iPhone, iPad and Mac in seconds.

Mixtapes

Make one by hand, albums and tracks in whatever order you like. Or make one from what you've played in the last hour. It's a proper tape, sides and all.

Send this tape

Send the tape in the deck as a link, from the share sheet or in Messages. They get a page with the cassette, its handwritten label and the tracks on each side. The music stays with you.

A demo tape

Until your music arrives there's one song in the deck, so you've got something to play from the first launch. After that it gets out of the way. Search for Glenn Callow and it's still there (yes, the song's called Andy Bateman).

Albums by their own names

Where a folder had to swap a colon or a slash for an underscore, the album shows its proper name from the tags.

Apple Music, as tapes

Pick Apple Music from the source button and your library swaps for your Apple Music one, catalogue search and all. The sound is Apple's own so the hiss and wear step aside. The reels and keys carry on. Needs a subscription.

Audiobooks and podcasts

Hook up your Audiobookshelf server and the deck plays your books and podcasts too. Each book's a tape, each chapter a track.

  • Your place is saved on your server, so you can carry on here or in Audiobookshelf's own app.
  • A book plays while it downloads. Start one hours in and it's playing in seconds.
  • 0.8× to 1.2× speed, same pitch.
  • 15 seconds back or 30 forward from the lock screen, your headphones or the car.
  • A stray tap won't lose your place. Only a proper wind moves a book's tape.

On your wrist

On Apple Watch you get the tape in your iPhone's deck, drawn the same way.

  • The same cassette, shell, label and reels, turning as it plays, with a counter that rolls along.
  • Tap the cassette to turn it over. Side B plays on from the same point.
  • Turn the crown for volume, same as Now Playing.
  • Chewed? The crown turns the pencil and winds the tape back in.
  • Your recent albums as cassette cases on a shelf, and your books with how far in you are.
  • A complication puts a cassette on your watch face.

In the car (and with Siri)

Tape Deck works with CarPlay. Pick from your music and books on the car's screen and your iPhone plays the tape.

  • Latest, Albums, Artists, Books and Podcasts. Latest has the newest episodes, what you played last and the books you're reading. Long lists come in runs of letters so you can get to everything while you drive.
  • Now Playing has the keys you'd expect: heart, shuffle, repeat, book speed and turn over. Up Next lists the tracks or chapters, tap one to play it.
  • Choose what shows on the car's Now Playing and your lock screen: the cassette window, the whole tape or the album art.
  • It picks up again after a call or the car's own announcements.
  • Ask Siri by name: "Play Rumours in Tape Deck". Albums, artists and books all work, so do "Carry on the tape in Tape Deck", "Play what's new in Tape Deck" and "Stop Tape Deck". They're all in Shortcuts too.

On iPhone, iPad and Mac

One app, at home on each.

  • Simple or Advanced. New installs start in Simple: plain keys, one side and only the settings that matter. Switch to Advanced at the top of tape settings for everything.
  • The first time round, a guide rings the buttons people ask about, once each. The help brings it back.
  • Handoff: open Tape Deck on another device and the tape carries on at the same track and second.
  • Turn the iPhone sideways and the cassette fills the screen. Haptics let you feel the keys and reels.
  • A Home Screen widget of the cassette, reels turning, keys underneath. Three sizes, plus the Lock Screen and StandBy.
  • Eight animated cassette stickers for Messages, each with something written on the label.
  • On the Mac: proper menus and shortcuts, a mini player that floats over your work and the media keys. The widget goes on the desktop and in Notification Centre too.
  • Right-click almost anything (the keys, the cassette, a row, the header buttons) for what you can do there. On a touchscreen, long press a row.
  • In a browser, save the page to your Home Screen and it works offline once it's been opened online. The tab title shows what's playing.
  • Every control is named for VoiceOver and a ring shows where the keyboard is.
  • An MX Creative Console plugin for the Mac too. The dial winds the tape, the keys go on the buttons. Built, not released yet.
  • Lock screen, Control Centre, AirPlay speakers (with the cassette kept in time with what you hear) and it carries on after a call.

Private by design

No account, no sign-in, no tracking. Tape Deck only asks for what you switch on: Apple Music if you pick it, your media library for Music on this iPhone and the motion sensor for the exploded view's tilt. Your music stays on your devices and your own server. Favourites go through your own iCloud.

Get it

It's free on the App Store for iPhone, iPad and Mac. The Apple Watch app comes with the iPhone one.

Want the newest bits first? Join the beta on TestFlight. Open the link on your iPhone, iPad or Mac, install TestFlight if it asks, then Accept and Install. Places are limited, so if it says it's full, try again later.

Needs iOS 18 or later, macOS 15 on the Mac and watchOS 11 on the watch.

Questions? The help starts with a numbered picture of the header, the keys and an album's row, then covers getting your music in, the settings and the questions testers have asked.
