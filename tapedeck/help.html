What the buttons are

The app's header, its keys and an album's row, each numbered. Find the button you're puzzled by, then read its number underneath.

The header

The heart and the circle are switches. They stay lit while they're on and a second press turns them off. The speakers and the source button stay lit while something other than the Mac or your own music is chosen. Every header button has a right-click menu that says what it does.

The keys

There are two sets, Tape and Modern. Choose in tape settings › Tape › Controls or right-click the keys. This is the Tape set, with the meters on.

The Modern set swaps these for a player's keys.

An album's row

A track has its own heart and download arrow, for that one track.

Getting started

Tape Deck plays music you already have. There are a few ways to bring it in and you can use them together.

Simple and Advanced
A new install starts in Simple: the modern keys, one side, no hiss or wear and only the settings that matter, which are Look and Library. Advanced has everything. Switch at the top of tape settings or from the gear's right-click menu (on the Mac, View › Simple and Advanced). What you set in Advanced is kept while you're in Simple and comes back as it was. Anyone who already had settings saved stays in Advanced.
First-run guide
The first time the player shows, a ring goes round the buttons people ask about, one at a time, with a card on what each does. Skip, Esc or Got it ends it. Show me around, in the help, brings it back.
Copy albums into the app
On an iPhone or iPad, open the Files app and copy album folders into On My iPhone › Tape Deck (or On My iPad), laid out as Artist › Album › tracks. On the Mac, choose File › Show Albums Folder and copy them in there. The app picks them up when you come back to it.
Through iCloud Drive
Copy album folders into iCloud Drive › Tape Deck › Music, laid out as Artist › Album › tracks. They show up in the app on every iPhone, iPad and Mac signed in to the same iCloud. An album fills in track by track as iCloud brings it to each device. It shows up while the app's open. An album folder dropped straight into Music, with no artist's folder around it, is named by its tags. Open the app once on a device for the Tape Deck folder to appear there.
Play from your own music server
If you run a music server, type its address in tape settings › Library › Music server or in its tile on the app's opening screen. It can be a Tape Deck Server, Plex (sign in with your Plex account), Jellyfin (with your username and password) or a Subsonic server such as Navidrome. The whole library on it is listed. A track downloads when you play it and the rest of its album follows. If the server asks for a password, a Password field shows. Start again, under the server in tape settings › Library, forgets the server and its sign-in and shows the opening screen again, with Open the player to come back.
No server? Run Tape Deck Server
Tape Deck Server is a free, open-source music server for the app: one Python file or one Docker container, on any computer, NAS or Raspberry Pi. Point it at a folder of albums laid out as Artist › Album › tracks, put it behind HTTPS (Caddy or Tailscale Serve will do) and type that address into the app. A password keeps it yours. A second one lets friends play without touching your favourites.
A music folder or a NAS
Tape Deck can play a folder where it is, without copying it in, laid out one folder per artist with one per album inside: tape settings › Library › Music folder › Choose or, on the Mac, File › Choose Music Folder… (⌘O). On an iPhone or iPad that can be a folder on a NAS. In the Files app or in the picker itself, tap ⋯ › Connect to Server, type the NAS's address (such as smb://192.168.1.20) and choose its music folder. Its albums take the place of the music server's copies. When the NAS or the drive can't be reached, its albums are left out until it can.
Songs on this iPhone or iPad
Songs you synced from a Mac or PC play as tapes once you turn on tape settings › Library › Music on this iPhone (or iPad). The system asks to use the media library then. Copy-protected songs and songs only in iCloud are left out and the app says how many. Apple Music's own downloads play in Apple Music mode instead. Not on the Mac.
Formats
MP3, AAC (M4A), FLAC and WAV. Ogg and Opus files are left out and the app says how many.

Playing

An album or an artist
Click the ▶ on its row or double-click the row to play it in order. Click a row to open it and see its tracks.
A row's menu
Right-click an album, book, episode or track row with a mouse. With a finger or an Apple Pencil, press and hold it for half a second. The menu has Play, Show or Hide tracks, This Tape, Favourite and the download. Moving, scrolling or a second finger cancels the press.
Two sets of keys
Choose in tape settings › Tape › Controls or right-click the keys. Tape is a portable recorder's: Play, Rewind, Fast forward, ■▲ and Pause. Modern is a player's: shuffle, skip back, one key to play and pause, skip forward, repeat and ■▲.
Play and Stop
Play latches down while the tape runs and its arrow points the way the tape's going: ▶ for side A, ◀ for side B. Press it while the tape is running to turn the tape over at the same point. ■▲ stops the tape where it is. Press it again to eject.
Pause
Holds a playing tape where it is, with Play still down. Press Pause again, Play or Space to carry on.
Rewind and fast forward
The tape races along the side until you press ■▲ (it stays there) or Play (it plays from there). With the modern keys, tap a skip key for the next or previous track or hold it to wind.
Wind to a spot
Click the bar in the player or the track index on the cassette's label and the reels race there. Click twice on a track to play it from the top. Drag the cassette sideways to wind it by hand.
A tape keeps its place
Put an album back in with its ▶ and it plays on from where it was when it came out: the same track and second. ▶ on the album already in the deck starts it from the top, as does a tape that was played to its end. Pick a track to start there instead. To have every tape start from the top, turn off tape settings › Tape › Each tape keeps its place.
This Tape
The list button in the player (or Q) shows the album that's playing, whole. Click a track to play it.
Mixtapes
On Recent, + Mixtape › New mixtape opens one. Name it, press + on the albums and tracks you want in the order you want them, then press Done. It plays as a tape of its own, sides and all. A tape of what I've played makes one from the last hour's tracks. Mixtapes are kept on the device they're made on.
Send this tape
The arrow at the top of This Tape sends the tape as a link, from the share sheet or from Tape Deck in Messages, which sends it beside the stickers. Whoever opens it sees the cassette, its label in its hand and its tracks a side. The music doesn't go with it. Nothing about the tape is kept anywhere. It's all in the link.
Handoff
With a music tape in the deck, your other iPhone, iPad or Mac, signed in to the same iCloud, offers Tape Deck (in the Dock or the app switcher). Open it there and the same track plays from the same second, further on by the time since if it was playing. The first device stops. If the other one doesn't have that album, it says so. Not for a book (its place follows through Audiobookshelf), a game or Apple Music.
A snapped tape
If a track can't be had when the tape reaches it (a lost connection, say) the tape pulls taut and snaps. Press Play to splice it and try again (no sticky tape needed). Or pick another track.
A chewed tape
At the Chewed end of the tape condition the deck now and then eats the tape. A loop falls out over the page. Press Play or tap the tape to wind it back in and the music carries on where it stopped. On Apple Watch, turn the Digital Crown to wind it in with the pencil. Turn the condition down if you'd rather it didn't.
With the screen off
Music keeps playing with the screen locked or the app in the background. The lock screen, Control Centre and headphone controls work. A call or an alarm stops the tape and it carries on afterwards. So does the car's announcement. Taking the headphones out or leaving the car stops it. The lock screen names each track's own artist, so a compilation's tracks say who each one is by.
AirPlay speakers
The speakers button in the header (a jack plug) lists your AirPlay speakers. Choose one and the tape plays there. The cassette is drawn a little late to keep time with what you hear.

The cassette

Show it
The cassette button or C. The expand button or Shift+F makes it full screen. On an iPhone, turn the phone sideways. The button beside it puts in a tape at random. Hold it to take the cassette apart or put it back together.
Choosing a tape
Tape settings › Look › Shell opens a grid of little cassettes. So does Choose a tape… at the top of the cassette's right-click menu. The grid holds the six shells, the eight classic tapes and 698 more, drawn from real cassettes of the 1970s to the 1990s. New ones can arrive without an update. Search by name or ID (blue 90, t12). Your favourites come first and the one in the deck is framed. Random, by album gives each album a tape of its own, the same one every time. A quick flick sideways across the label puts in the next tape. A slow drag still winds.
Label styles and handwriting
Look › Label style prints the label in one of thirteen styles, the one the shell came with or Random. Look › Handwriting has eight hands: System, Biro, Felt tip, Marker, Neat, Scrawl, Capitals (block letters) and Typed. Random gives each album one of them except Typed, the same every time.
Taking it apart
Look has a clear shell, the label off and a cutaway. Exploded view pulls the whole cassette into its layers. Apart, the layers tilt a little as you move the mouse or a pen over the cassette or tilt the phone. On an iPhone the first tap asks permission to read its tilt. It stays still under Reduce Motion.
What a side holds
One track, the whole album or the whole queue (tape settings › Tape › Tape holds). Two halves splits it over sides A and B. A C46 to a C120 cuts the sides to that length. Fit to content keeps it all on one side.
Auto-reverse
At the end of side A the deck plays side B backwards without taking the tape out. With it off, the tape is turned over between sides. D turns it over at any time.
Track or Tape view
The switch in the player or T. Track shows the track playing. Tape shows the whole tape, side after side, with the leader and any blank tape at the end.
VU meters
Tape settings › Look › VU meters or a right-click on the cassette. Two rows of twelve lights in the transport beside the bar, one for each channel, green, then amber, then red as the tape gets louder. They show the tape's level, not the volume's. Apple Music's sound never reaches the deck, so they're off there.
Tape settings
The gear in the header or on the cassette (or G) opens them. Tape: what a side holds, side length, auto-reverse, the keys and the leader. Sound: the tape's condition, its type and Dolby, the cue tone and motor wow. Look: the shell, the label style, the handwriting, the VU meters and the tint from the cover. Library: the music server, the audiobook server, the downloads and Reset, which puts the settings back to their defaults and keeps your favourites and your place. In Simple, only Look and Library are there.

Your library

Views
The tabs above the list are Recent, Albums, Artists and Tracks. Recent is the ten albums you listened to last, then the ten newest in your library.
Sort
The button beside the search names the order the list is in. Click it for the view's columns. Choose the one already in use to turn the order round. Recent has its own order and no button.
Search
Searches title, artist and album as you type. Every word has to match somewhere and accents don't need typing. Esc or the cross in the box clears it. On a phone the cassette steps aside while the keyboard is up, so the box and what it finds sit just under the header. It comes back when you tap Done.
Favourites
The heart on a track marks it. The heart on an album's row marks the whole album. The heart in the header, Favourites only, shows nothing but favourites. They follow you between your iPhone, iPad and Mac through iCloud.
What's on this device
A yellow dot means an album or track is on this device. A ring means part of the album is. The Downloaded only button in the header, a ring with a dot, shows only what's on this device. When the server can't be reached, everything else is dimmed and skipped.
Downloads
The download button beside an album's ▶ fetches it without playing it. The × removes the downloaded copy (music you copied in yourself is never removed). A track has its own download button beside its heart, for that one track. Tape settings › Library › Downloads sets how much space to keep, 20 GB unless you change it. Past that, the albums played longest ago are removed first. The Download button there fetches every favourite. So does a long press on the header's heart. While anything downloads, a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island shows what's coming (a book's title or an album and its artist), a ring filling by the bytes in and a count of how many are still to go. On iPhone and iPad.

Apple Music

Going in
Choose Apple Music from the source button in the header and your music swaps for your Apple Music library. Music Library swaps it back. The first time, the app asks to use Apple Music. Playing needs an Apple Music subscription.
What's listed
Recent (what Apple Music played last), Albums, Artists, Songs and Playlists, each playlist as an album of its songs. The search looks through your library at once and, after a pause, through the Apple Music catalogue, whose albums and songs come under a heading of their own.
The sound
An album, a playlist or a song is a tape. The reels, the counter and the keys run as ever. The sound is Apple Music's own, so the tape's hiss, wow, wear and cue tone are off while you're there and come back when you leave. The keys, the winding and the eject still sound. Nothing is downloaded.
Hearts
A heart on an Apple Music album is an ordinary favourite. The same album in your own music shares it.

Audiobooks and podcasts

Connecting
Put your Audiobookshelf server's address and an API key in tape settings › Library and Audiobooks appears in the menu of the source button in the header. It swaps the music for your books and podcasts. Music Library swaps them back.
Playing a book
A book is a tape and its chapters are the tracks. It plays while it downloads, so a book or episode you start hours in plays within seconds, not after the whole file. Skip past what has arrived and the tape holds taut, saying how much is still to come. Play takes it up where you left off, in this app or Audiobookshelf's own. Entering the books puts the one you played last in the deck, ready for Play.
Podcasts
Your Audiobookshelf podcast libraries are shows. A show opens to its episodes, newest first. Each episode is a tape with its own place, download and speed. Latest episodes gathers the newest across every show. Skip on one of its rows marks the episode played on the server and takes it off the list. A show's ▶ plays its newest from where you left it.
Speed and skips
The menu left of Play reads 0.8× to 1.2× at the same pitch. The lock screen, the headphones and the car skip back 15 seconds and on 30.
Without the server
A book that's wholly downloaded plays from the last place this device knew when the server can't be reached (on a plane, say). What you hear goes up when the server's back. A newer place from another device stands.
Keeping your place
So a stray tap can't lose your place, a book's tape moves only by Rewind and Fast forward, the bar in Track view or a chapter's row.

Apple Watch

Getting it
The watch app comes with the iPhone app. If it isn't on your watch, open the Watch app on your iPhone and install Tape Deck from the list there. The iPhone plays the music. The watch shows the tape and works the keys.
The deck
The cassette in your iPhone's deck, in the same shell, with the album's label in the same hand. The reels turn and the counter rolls as the tape plays. Under the cassette are the side and the track, then the keys: back, Play, on and Stop. Play stays down while the tape runs. Tap the cassette to turn it over where it is. The other side plays on from the same point, with side B forwards under its own letter.
The Digital Crown
Turn it to set your iPhone's volume. A bar beside the crown shows the level as it changes. With the tape chewed, it turns the pencil instead.
A chewed tape
The loop hangs out of the cassette with a pencil in the hub. Turn the crown to wind it back in. Once it's all in, the music carries on.
Recent and Books
Swipe left for the albums you played last, as cassette cases on a shelf. Swipe again for the books you're reading. Tap one to play it.
The complication
Touch and hold your watch face, tap Edit and choose Tape Deck for one of its complications. It shows a cassette and, where there's room, what's playing. Tap it to open the app.

Widget and stickers

The Home Screen widget
On an iPhone or iPad, touch and hold the Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget and choose Tape Deck. It shows the cassette in the deck in small, medium or large, with its hubs turning while the tape plays. Its keys (Previous, Play or Stop, then Next) work without opening the app. It goes on the Lock Screen and, on an iPhone, in StandBy too. Tap it to open the app.
On the Mac
Right-click the desktop, choose Edit Widgets and add Tape Deck. It's the same cassette in its three sizes, with the same keys, on the desktop and in Notification Centre. With another window in front the desktop draws it in lines.
iMessage stickers
In a conversation in Messages, tap + and choose Tape Deck (under More if it isn't listed). There are eight cassettes, their hubs turning, each labelled in a hand with something to send: Mixtape, Road Trip, Love Songs, Thank You, Miss You, Happy Birthday, Good Night and Mind Blown. Tap one to send it or touch and hold it to stick it on a message.

In the car and Siri

CarPlay
Connect your iPhone and choose Tape Deck on the car's screen. The tabs are Latest (the newest episodes you haven't finished, the albums you listened to last and the books you're reading, five of each), Albums (your favourites and the five newest first, then the letters), Artists and, with an Audiobookshelf server, Books and Podcasts. A car that takes fewer tabs puts Albums and Artists under one, Music. While the car's moving a long list comes in runs (A–C, D–F and so on) and, within a letter, by name, so all of it can be reached.
Now Playing
The car's Now Playing shows the cassette, with keys for what's in the deck: the heart, shuffle and repeat for music, a book's speed and turn over, five at most. Up Next lists the tape's tracks (a book's chapters) with the one playing marked. A tap plays that one. The artist's name opens their albums. Its picture, also on your lock screen, is set in tape settings › Look › Now Playing picture (Advanced): the cassette's window, the whole tape on the album's colours, the whole tape wide or the album art.
Siri and Shortcuts
By name: "Play Rumours in Tape Deck" plays an album, an artist or a book. Siri knows the names in your library, the books you're reading included, so a new album may take a little while to be heard. The older three still work: "Carry on the tape in Tape Deck" (or "Play Tape Deck") plays the tape in the deck from where it stopped, a book too. "Play what's new in Tape Deck" plays the album that came into your library last. "Stop Tape Deck" stops it. All of them are in the Shortcuts app as well.

Mini player
A small window that stays above the others and shows just the cassette. Move the mouse over it for the controls.
Right-click

The rest of the header's buttons have one too: the source button's menu, the speakers, the settings, the help and Downloaded only. Each ends with About Tape Deck, which shows the version.

Questions

What people have asked while testing Tape Deck.

Getting started

The app opens on "Where your music comes from"

That's the opening screen. It shows when there's nothing to play yet and whenever you ask for it in tape settings › Library › Where the music comes from. Each source has a tile saying what it needs and how it stands: your own music (iCloud Drive › Tape Deck › Music, the app's own folder through the Files app, File › Show Albums Folder on the Mac or a folder played where it is), a Tape Deck Server, Plex, Jellyfin or another Subsonic server, Audiobookshelf and Apple Music. Tap one to set it up there. A folder you make yourself, with the app's folder's name, isn't read. Use the app's own, which Open in Files takes you to. Meanwhile the demo tape plays. It's a song by Glenn Callow (yes, it's called Andy Bateman). Once music of your own comes it stays out of the lists and a search for either name finds it. With no server of your own, Tape Deck Server serves a folder of music to the app.

Where do I get it?

Tape Deck's on the App Store for iPhone, iPad and Mac. It's free, with the Apple Watch app inside the iPhone one. A beta, with the newest changes first, is on TestFlight for iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Everything's gone plain: where did the settings go?

You're in Simple, which puts away what a newcomer has no use for. Open tape settings and choose Advanced at the top. Your Advanced settings are kept while you're in Simple.

Does the page work offline?

The web version, saved to the Home Screen, opens with no network once you've opened it online (it needs https). It keeps the page and the covers it has seen, never the music, so tracks still need the server unless they're downloaded in an app. The browser tab's title says what's playing. Neither applies to the apps.

Which iPhone or iOS does it need?

iOS 18 or later for the iPhone and iPad, macOS 15 for the Mac and watchOS 11 for the Apple Watch. On something older the app won't install, so update the system first.

The invitation email's sign-in loops

Open the invitation on the iPhone itself, install TestFlight from the App Store and choose View in TestFlight (or Start Testing) in the email. If the sign-in still loops, open the same link on a Mac or in a private browser window. An invitation runs out after a few days, so ask for a new one if yours has.

TestFlight emailed me a build number that doesn't match

The iPhone and Mac apps are built separately and each has its own build number. Check which app the email names. A Mac build isn't an update for the iPhone.

It went black after a while in the background

iOS can end the app's page to free memory while it's in the background and older builds were left with nothing to show. The app loads the page again when you come back, so if it happens, update it from the App Store.

The watch shows an empty deck

The watch draws what's in your iPhone's deck, so open Tape Deck on the iPhone. With both in reach of each other, the tape appears on the watch within a few seconds.

How do I make it sound clean?

In tape settings › Sound, set the condition to Fresh and turn off the cue tone and motor wow. In Tape, turn off the lead in and lead out. It'll sound like a CD, which rather defeats the point, but it's your tape.

Does it collect anything about me?

No. There's no account and no tracking. The app asks only for what you switch on. That's Apple Music (the first time you choose it from the source button), your media library (for Music on this iPhone) and the motion sensor (for the exploded view's tilt). Your music stays on your devices and your own servers. Your favourites go through your own iCloud. The privacy policy says what the app keeps and where.

Servers and sources

Can I use Spotify?

No. Spotify isn't a source Tape Deck can play. It plays Apple Music (with a subscription) and music you have as files: copied into the app, in a folder or on your own music server.

Does Tape Deck stream from a music service? Do I need an Apple Music subscription?

Apple Music, with a subscription. Choose Apple Music from the source button in the header and it plays your Apple Music library and the catalogue as tapes. Playing needs the subscription, even for songs already in your library. Without one, Tape Deck plays music you have as files. More on Apple Music.

Should I have the book icon?

That's the source button. It's only there once there's a second source: an Audiobookshelf server (tape settings › Library) or Apple Music. With only your own music, the header has no source button. It's number 3 in the picture of the header.

Can I point it at my Jellyfin, Plex or Navidrome?

Yes. Type the address in tape settings › Library › Music server. Jellyfin and Navidrome need your username and password, typed under the address. Jellyfin is spoken to directly, with no plugin. For Plex, sign in with your Plex account. The music server Tape Deck was made for is Tape Deck Server.

I chose a big music folder and it says "reading…"

A folder of thousands of tracks takes a few minutes to read, longer over a network. A bar under it shows how far it's got: the files found, then each track, then the covers. Its albums are listed as they're read. What's been read is kept if you put the app away, so it carries on from there and the next time it's quick. If you choose iTunes Media (or the Music app's Media folder), its Music folder is read and the podcasts and films beside it are left out.

My server asks for a password

The first screen shows a Password field when the server asks. A Tape Deck Server's sign-in is kept for 30 days and asked for again when it runs out (from version 4.20.1). A Subsonic server takes a username and password. If you've forgotten it, Start again, in tape settings › Library, forgets the server and its sign-in and shows the opening screen.

The first screen says Connecting… and never stops

That was a server asking for its password while the password field was empty. Connect asked again and was asked again. From version 4.35.1, with the password wanted and the field empty, Connect says so. Type the password or choose Start again if you meant a different server.

It says "That server didn't answer"

Either the address can't be reached from where you are or the server wants a password and the app was never given one. Open the address in Safari to see which. If it asks for a password, the first screen has the field for it (version 4.20.1 and later).

Can I play my music from a NAS?

Yes, two ways. On the iPhone or iPad, connect the NAS in the Files app (⋯ › Connect to Server, such as smb://192.168.1.20), then choose its music folder in tape settings › Library › Music folder › Choose (version 4.30.0 and later). Or run a music server on it and type its address. The folder must hold one folder per artist, with one per album inside.

What you see

What do the icons in the header mean?

From the left: the cassette mark and the title, the count, the source button, the heart (favourites only), the circle (downloaded only), the speakers, the settings and the help. The numbered picture at the top of this help says what each does.

Why do some buttons stay lit?

The heart and the circle are switches. They stay lit while they're on and a second press turns them off. The source button is lit while you're away from your own music. The speakers are lit while a speaker is chosen. On a touch screen a button can also look lit after a tap until you touch something else. That's the screen's own hover left standing, not a setting. It's mended from the build after 4.35.3.

What do the heart and the download arrow on an album do?

The heart marks the whole album as a favourite. The heart in the header then shows favourites only. The arrow downloads the album to this device without playing it. A ring or a yellow dot where the arrow was says how much of it is here. The picture of an album's row has each state.

What are the yellow dots and rings?

A dot means the album or track is on this device and a ring means part of it is. The circle in the header shows only what's on this device and counts the tracks still to come while downloads run.

Does it work with VoiceOver?

Every control has a name, a lit toggle says it's on and a ring shows where the keyboard is. The list has one Tab stop, with the arrow keys, Home, End, Return and Space. Each row reads as its album, artist and track count. The bar says its place in words. A line you can't see says what happened with no tap: the tape snapping or eaten, the downloads done, the server out of reach and back.

My favourites aren't on my other device

They go through iCloud, so check you're signed in to iCloud with the same Apple Account on both. They usually arrive within a minute.
